Featured install

Pair of new doors — full driveway swap

Two-door jobs price differently than one. Door pricing scales close to linearly, but springs, openers, and labour see a small discount when both bays are done in a single visit. We quote each combination separately on site, in writing, before any work begins.

Why prices vary between homes

Door size and weight. A heavy wood-look carriage door on Crossing Bridge Way needs different springs and rollers than a standard steel double-car in Jackson Trails. More weight means higher-rated springs, more labour to balance, and sometimes upgraded hardware.

Opener brand and age. LiftMaster and Chamberlain belt-drive units from the late 2000s share common wear parts we stock. Older Genie screw-drive models or discontinued boards may need more time to source — we tell you before we order.

Parts quality. We can match OEM-grade springs and rollers or explain when a budget part is appropriate on an older door you plan to replace within a year. You choose after you see the quote — not after the work is half done.

What else failed at the same time. A spring snap often stresses cables and drums. A bent track may have damaged rollers and hinges. We list everything on the written quote so you are not surprised by “while we were here” add-ons.

What drives the price up

  • Three-car doors and custom carriage panels in Crossing Bridge Estates
  • High-cycle or oversized torsion springs on oversized double doors
  • Jackshaft or wall-mount opener installs where ceiling clearance is tight
  • Multiple failed components (spring + cable + drum) from continuing to run a crooked door
  • After-hours emergency dispatch between 10 PM and 7 AM — surcharge quoted before we roll out

Cable repair detail

Wayne Dalton retainer — full bottom-end rebuild

Wayne Dalton doors use a proprietary bottom retainer for the seal — it must be removed and reset correctly any time the cable or roller is replaced. On this Stittsville job we fitted a new bottom weather seal, lift cable, nylon roller, and bottom bracket in one visit. The retainer hardware and seal alignment time pushes this job to the higher end of cable-repair pricing.

What drives the price down

  • Single-component fixes (sensor alignment, limit adjustment, one roller set)
  • Standard-size doors with parts on the truck — common in Fairwinds and Wyldewood steel setups
  • Single-component fixes completed in one visit without extra trip charges
  • Annual maintenance tune-ups that prevent opener and cable failures later

Phone quotes vs on-site quotes

We can give ranges for common jobs if you describe door size and symptoms — a loud bang usually means spring repair ($250–$350); a humming motor with no movement often means opener repair ($150–$300). We confirm the final number in writing on site before work starts.

We will not quote you a final price over the phone for a complex repair, because we would be guessing. We can share the typical range from the table below, then confirm the exact number in writing on site after we inspect the door — free estimate, no dispatch fee.

Compare jobs side by side

Homeowners often ask whether to repair or replace. If a Fairwinds door from 1998 has a failed spring but sound panels and tracks, spring repair plus a tune-up is usually the right economics. If Amberwood steel is rusted through the bottom section and the springs are original, new door installation may cost less over five years than repeated panel patches. We walk through that math on site — with real numbers from the table above, not a sales script.

Opener repair ($150–$300) makes sense when the rail, motor, and door are sound. New opener installation ($650–$950 installed) makes sense when the unit is obsolete, loud, or missing modern safety features. Panel repair ($150–$400 in place) makes sense when the steel is still sound; we will say so if a full door swap is smarter.

Opener install

New Chamberlain chain-drive opener

Chain-drive units are the budget-friendly workhorse — durable and well-suited to detached garages or homes where opener noise is not a concern. For Stittsville garages that share a wall with a bedroom (common in Jackson Trails splits) we recommend stepping up to a belt-drive instead. Installed pricing covers the unit, rail, safety sensors, wall console, remotes, and a full safety and auto-reverse test before we hand over the remotes.
